A.2 Affordability calculations

Maximum affordable rent is 30% of gross monthly income. Maximum affordable purchase price is the loan a household can carry at a 28% front-end ratio, after a fixed monthly allowance for taxes and insurance, divided by one minus the down payment (a 10% down payment means the loan covers 90% of the price). The mortgage rate is the latest monthly PMMS 30-year average. The same assumptions produce the income needed to buy the median home (Chapter 5). An earlier version of this calculation multiplied the loan by 0.9 instead of dividing by it, which understated affordable prices by about a fifth; it was corrected in October 2026.

A.3 Town-level home prices

Town prices in the ownership chapter come from Bright MLS’s published town-area statistics for Warrenton and Bealeton. Annual figures are sales-weighted averages of monthly medians. The ZIP codes that carry the town names (20186/20187 and 22712) cover large rural areas outside the towns and run higher; they are noted for context only.

A.4 CoStar rent types

CoStar classifies each property’s rent type. Properties marked Affordable or Market/Affordable (rent-restricted, subsidized or mixed-income) are grouped as affordable; the rest are market-rate. Most of the units CoStar tracks with quarterly rents are at affordable properties. Rents in the rental chapter are shown in current dollars; inflation comparisons use CPI-U less shelter.

A.5 Population series

Two population estimates appear in this report, a year apart. The demographics chapter (Chapter 2) leads with the U.S. Census Bureau Population Estimates Program (PEP) July 1, 2025 estimate (Vintage 2025) — 76,503 — because PEP is the current, point-in-time measure best suited to describing recent growth.

The projections chapter (Chapter 7) bases its household-formation and forward-need estimates on the ACS 2020–2024 5-year estimate — 74,577 (Table B01003) — because the projections model is built on ACS-derived household size (2.78, B25010) and CHAS affordability shares, which are themselves 5-year-pooled measures. Using the same ACS universe keeps the inputs internally consistent. These are different measures of the same underlying population (PEP is a point-in-time July estimate; ACS 5-year is a period estimate pooling surveys from roughly 2022 in concept), so the ~1,900-person gap (about 1,300 against the PEP 2024 estimate; PEP is a year ahead of the ACS period) is expected. Readers comparing the two chapters should not assume one figure is wrong.

A.6 ACS estimates labeled by a single year

All figures and tables in this report that cite a single calendar year for American Community Survey data (e.g., “2024”) are drawn from ACS 5-year estimates referenced by their end year. The “2024” estimates pool survey responses collected from 2020 through 2024.

Five-year estimates are used throughout because Fauquier County and especially its two places (Warrenton, Bealeton) have populations too small for reliable 1-year ACS estimates. Readers should not interpret a “2024” ACS figure as a point-in-time 2024 value; it reflects conditions across the full five-year survey window, with the center of the period near 2022.

A.7 Bright MLS and CoStar vintages

Bright MLS transaction exports run through 10/30/2026. Annual sales counts for 2016–2021 come from the Greater Piedmont REALTOR / VAR series; 2022 onward come from the Bright MLS closed-sales exports. 2026 is a partial year (821 closings through October); any 2026 comparison is made against the same months of 2025.

Two active-listing snapshots are drawn from the same 10/30/2026 export and kept separate: 267 active for-sale listings (used for the listing counts in the ownership and affordability-gap chapters) and 41 active rental listings (used only for the asking-rent comparison in the rental chapter). Rental listings are a small, single-day snapshot; read them as directional.

Monthly summary series (published Bright MLS summaries, Fauquier, Warrenton, Bealeton) run January 2022 through October 2026. The final month is partial and is labeled as such where it is quoted.

Published vs. transaction-derived counts. The published monthly summary reports roughly 17–23% more sales than the transaction-level exports (a property-type filter in the exports). Median sale prices agree to about 0.1%. Sales volume is charted from the transaction export and the monthly summary is used for supply and days-on-market; neither source is re-based on the other, and the active-listing snapshot carries the same narrower definition as the export.

CoStar multifamily quarterly data run 2016 Q1 through 2026 Q3, covering 14 tracked apartment properties (about 998 units) within a broader inventory of 41 multifamily properties. CoStar covers professionally managed apartments only and is supplemental to the ACS rent anchor used for affordability.
